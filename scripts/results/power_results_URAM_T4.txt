URAM K=4 achieves a modest throughput improvement over hybrid K=2 at the same
clock (250 MHz). The 5.8% decode improvement is below the projected 25% (2.6 t/s),
suggesting the practical bottleneck has shifted from FFN compute time (~68% of
total) further toward CPU-side attention/norms (~126 ms/token). Per Amdahl's
Law, reducing FFN time from ~305 ms to ~230 ms only improves end-to-end from
~431 ms to ~356 ms — a 17% reduction in FFN time yields ~5-6% end-to-end gain.

Power is slightly lower than hybrid K=2 (4.83 W vs 5.01 W), consistent with
URAM replacing LUTRAM-heavy mux trees. Thermal margins are healthy with all
temperatures under 30 C.
