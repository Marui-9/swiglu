\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}
\label{cha:conclusioni}

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work presented a selective FPGA offload strategy for LFM2.5-1.2B, a model
co-designed for CPU inference~\cite{amini2025lfm2technicalreport}, targeting the MatMul +
SwiGLU feed-forward block to enable conversational audio inference pipelines on
resource-constrained edge platforms. The implementation integrates with the existing
llama.cpp/ggml flow through a fused operator, and is faster than CPU Only execution of
the same model at every thread count: 2.09$\times$ in decode at one thread, and 3.53
against 2.69\,t/s when the best configuration of each path is compared. It also uses less
energy per decoded token at every thread count, and reaches sub-500\,ms decode latency
with two CPU threads instead of three.

These speeds are reported together with their accuracy. Validating the output against
the CPU path exposed three driver bugs that throughput measurements alone had not
revealed, and a fixed INT8 range for the intermediate products that cost a mean KL
divergence of 0.385. With the bugs fixed and the intermediate products widened to INT16,
the accelerated model scores a mean KL divergence of 0.030 and a perplexity ratio of
1.034 against CPU Only.

At the architectural level, overlapping the weight loads with the MAC arrays turned both
projection stages from load-bound into MAC-bound, and cut the csynth call latency from
3.18M to 2.02M cycles. On the board the overlap of Stages~2a/2b took effect only after
the loop-body synchronization that HLS had inserted was removed; a call now takes
8.09\,ms, within 1\% of its csynth latency, against 11.34\,ms before. The design
operates near the usable logic and routing budget of the ZU5EV (86.7\% LUT) and near the
bandwidth of its HP ports in Stages~2a/2b, so further gains are less likely to come
from parallel replication than from reducing the CPU work outside the offloaded
block. Overall, the study confirms that selective feed-forward acceleration is
a viable step toward conversational-level edge inference, and provides a concrete
roadmap for the next optimizations in LFM2.5-Audio-1.5B deployment.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future}

\textbf{Memory bandwidth.} On the board, the gate-weight port of Stages~2a/2b reads at
3.68\,GB/s, 92\% of the 4.0\,GB/s peak of a 128-bit port at 250\,MHz, and Stage~5 is
bound by its 16-lane MAC array. Memory-bound behaviour is typical of autoregressive
decoding~\cite{FlightLLM}, and the IP has reached that regime: a faster Stage~2 needs
more or wider ports into the DDR, and a faster Stage~5 needs the wider output array that
does not route on the ZU5EV. The DDR controller's performance monitor, which located the
board/csynth gap of the first overlapped build, is the tool to check any such change.

\textbf{Numerical representation.} With INT16 $X_1$/$X_2$, the remaining difference from
the CPU output comes from the INT8 quantization of the input $x$ and of the gated
activation $h$ (per-layer cosine 0.9930 at worst). Wider $x$ and $h$, or a per-token
scale for $X_1$/$X_2$ instead of the fixed $\pm 32$ range, are the next steps; the
latter needs a second pass over $X_1$/$X_2$ and was therefore not taken. Against an
f16/Q8\_0 version of the same model (24 chunks of wikitext-2), the uniform Q4\_K format
scores a mean KL divergence of 0.166 on the CPU, against 0.153 for the default Q4\_K\_M
recipe that keeps eight down projections in Q6\_K, and the accelerator raises it to 0.210
(Table~\ref{tab:accuracy_ref}). The INT8 $x$ and $h$ thus add 0.044 to what the 4-bit
weights already cost (derived).

\textbf{CPU work outside the offloaded block.} At two threads, the non-offloaded part of
the graph takes about 211\,ms per token, more than the 131\,ms of the offloaded
feed-forward path, and it caps decode at about 4.7\,t/s even with an instantaneous
accelerator. Each feed-forward call blocks the graph until the UIO interrupt arrives.
Within one token this cannot be hidden, because each layer's output feeds the next, but
it could be with independent work, such as several concurrent sequences. Other
co-designed frameworks achieve more stable scaling through tighter compiler--runtime
integration~\cite{cano}. Future work will investigate asynchronous execution and
pipelined scheduling along these lines.

\textbf{Hardware coverage and platform.} Extending the offload to the final linear
projection and the gated short convolution would attack the CPU share directly, but the
feed-forward block alone already uses 86.7\% of the LUTs, and timing closes with only
$+0.145$\,ns of slack. Similar work pushing the same platform to its limit required
complex operator fusion and custom data arrangements to maximize memory
bandwidth~\cite{Li2025Pushing}. A larger device of the same family, such as the ZU7EV,
would provide room for a wider output MAC array, which does not route on the ZU5EV, and
for further kernels.

\textbf{DSP usage.} The design uses 19\% of the DSP slices. One multiply per MAC lane
already maps to a DSP48E2, so the low figure reflects the small number of lanes rather
than a missed mapping. More lanes in Stages~2a/2b would wait on the same port bandwidth.
In Stage~5, the lane count was halved to fund the overlap buffers, so restoring it
depends on making the four-row load shorter than the MAC, not on DSP availability.

Overall, future improvements will require co-optimization of data movement, scheduling,
and numerical representation, as well as evaluation on platforms with higher bandwidth
and resources, bridging the gap between lightweight selective acceleration and more
general co-design frameworks.
