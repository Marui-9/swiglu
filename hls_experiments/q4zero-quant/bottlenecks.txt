Bottleneck Analysis — Q4_0 K_WV=12 + Time-Mux REDUCE Architecture
====================================================================
Date: 2026-05-23  |  Based on: csynth.rpt (May 23, K_WV=12 run)
HLS: 81,751 LUTs (69%), 148 DSP (11%), 206 BRAM (71%), 221K FF (94%)
Vivado: ~109K LUTs (93%), routes and closes timing at 250 MHz
Top-level: 3.99M cycles, 2.63 t/s projected


1. Output Path — 1.54M cycles (38.6% of total, largest single block)
---------------------------------------------------------------------
The compute_output phase is serialized after gate and consumes 1.54M cycles.
Within it, DDR load dominates:

    load_mg_down_q40: 470 cyc/MG × 8 MG = 3,760 cyc/iter  (63% of output iter)
    mac_mg_down_q40:  273 cyc/MG × 8 MG = 2,184 cyc/iter  (37% of output iter)

The load is at the AXI bandwidth ceiling (128 bits/cycle, II=1).  Attempted
fixes:
  - K_DOWN=16: doubled parallelism, halved iterations (256→128).  Failed
    Vivado at 130.8K LUTs (111.7%) — REDUCE_MG at 16 parallel DSP routes
    inflated LUTs super-linearly.
  - Output time-mux REDUCE: reduced REDUCE_MG from 8 to 1 DSP (8,418→3,187
    HLS LUTs).  But REDUCE(64 cyc) > MAC_MG(35 cyc) so overlap can't hide
    REDUCE — output cycles increased 1.54M→1.83M (+19%).  Reverted.

The output path remains the most impactful untapped lever.  A successful
K_DOWN=16 implementation would save ~340K cycles, pushing t/s to ~2.9.
This requires solving the REDUCE_MG routing inflation — either a different
time-mux structure for the output path (where MAC_MG > REDUCE_MG, flipped
from current) or DSP cascade optimization specific to the output path.


2. DDR Load Bandwidth — Saturated at 128 bits/cycle
-----------------------------------------------------
Both WV and output load phases are at the AXI ceiling:

    WV load:   1,110 cyc/iter × 683 iter = 0.76M cycles (19% of total)
    Out load:  3,760 cyc/iter × 256 iter = 0.96M cycles (24% of total)
    Combined:  1.72M cycles (43% of total) spent just moving data from DDR

    Effective bandwidth per port: 128 bits/cycle × 250 MHz = 4.0 GB/s peak.
    Achieved: ~3.9 GB/s (97.5% utilization) during load phases.

The ZU5EV's HP ports are 128-bit max.  Cannot widen.  The ports are idle
during MAC phases (gmem_Wd idle for 2.43M cycles during X1/X2).  Overlapping
W_down pre-load with X1/X2 compute could hide some output load latency but
requires major DATAFLOW restructuring (gate_cache not ready until gate
completes, so only DDR reads can be pre-loaded, not MAC).


3. LUT Budget — 93% Used, the Binding Constraint
--------------------------------------------------
Vivado LUTs at ~109K of 117K leaves ~8K headroom.  This is the primary
resource ceiling.  Every K_WV increase adds ~7K HLS LUTs per instance
(mostly in load mux + MAC_ALL routing), or ~18K Vivado.  K_WV=16 proved
II=1 achievable at 16 chains but required 135K Vivado LUTs (−18K over).

Where LUTs go (HLS, per compute_X1 instance):
    load_12_rows:     8,084  (16% — AXI address computation, 12-way mux)
    MAC_ALL:          2,478  ( 5% — 12 parallel nibble extracts, zero-LUT)
    REDUCE_GRP:       5,146  (10% — time-muxed, 1 DSP, float→fixed converter)
    Other MAC infra:  2,615  ( 5% — ZERO_DSP, ping-pong buffers, QUANTIZE)
    ─────────────────────────
    Total X1:        26,466  (×2 instances = 52,932)
    compute_gate:     6,397  (13%)
    compute_output:  17,125  (34%)
    load_x_local:       172  (~0%)

DSPs (12%) and URAM (12.5%) have massive headroom but cannot substitute for
routing LUTs.  The load function's AXI address computation (64-bit multiply
for base address calculation) and the 12-way LOAD_NIB demux are the largest
single LUT consumers that could theoretically be reduced, but no clear path
to do so within HLS constraints.

The narrow-REDUCE optimization (fxd_scale_t 32→18 bits) was the biggest LUT
win in this design: REDUCE_GRP dropped from 11,755→5,146 HLS LUTs per
instance (−56%), REDUCE_MG from 9,626→8,418 (−13%).  This single change
enabled K_WV=12 to fit.


4. K_WV Scaling Ceiling — II=1 Holds but LUTs Don't
-----------------------------------------------------
K_WV=12: MAC_ALL II=1 at 12 chains, 260 cycles, fits Vivado (109K LUTs).
K_WV=16: MAC_ALL II=1 at 16 chains, 260 cycles, FAILS Vivado (135K LUTs).

The fabric can sustain II=1 at 16 parallel BRAM reads + 16 DSP routes at
250 MHz (confirmed by HLS synthesis).  But the resource cost per additional
chain (~1,800 HLS LUTs for nibble extraction + load mux + REDUCE routing)
pushes the design over the LUT budget.

K_WV=12 is the practical ZU5EV ceiling for this architecture with current
optimizations.  A larger device (ZU7EV with 230K LUTs) would enable K_WV=16
and ~2.82 t/s.


5. Amdahl's Law — CPU Overhead Limits Returns
-----------------------------------------------
The SwiGLU FFN is ~68% of total token processing time.  CPU overhead
(attention, layer norms, embedding, sampling, driver) is ~125 ms/token.

    Current FPGA time:  255 ms (16 layers × 15.96 ms)
    Total per token:    255 + 125 = 380 ms
    Throughput:         1 / 0.380 = 2.63 t/s

    Theoretical max at infinite FPGA speed:  1 / 0.125 = 8.0 t/s
    With FPGA at 0 ms:                      8.0 t/s

    To reach 3.0 t/s:  FPGA ≤ 1000/3.0 − 125 = 208 ms → 13.0 ms/layer
                       → 3.25M cycles (current: 3.99M, gap: −18.6%)

    To reach 3.5 t/s:  FPGA ≤ 1000/3.5 − 125 = 161 ms → 10.0 ms/layer
                       → 2.50M cycles (current: 3.99M, gap: −37%)

Each incremental t/s costs exponentially more in FPGA performance due to
the fixed CPU component.  Going from 2.63→3.0 requires −740K cycles;
2.63→3.5 requires −1.49M cycles.  The latter is likely infeasible on ZU5EV.


6. DATAFLOW Sequential Staging — No Token Pipelining
------------------------------------------------------
The 5 DATAFLOW stages process one token at a time.  Stage N+1 cannot start
until stage N completes.  Total latency = sum of all stages.

    load_x:          202 cyc
    max(X1,X2):  2,434,896 cyc   ← bottleneck (61%)
    gate:           16,553 cyc
    output:      1,539,407 cyc   ← second largest (39%)
    Total:       3,991,058 cyc

Token-level pipelining (e.g., token N+1's X1/X2 overlapping with token N's
output) could reduce per-token latency to max(stage) ≈ 2.43M instead of
sum(stage) ≈ 3.99M, a 39% reduction.  But this requires double-buffering
all intermediate storage (X1_cache, X2_cache, gate_cache, out_local) —
approximately doubling BRAM usage.  Not implemented due to complexity and
the fact that single-token decode (ne[1]=1) is the only supported mode.


7. Fixed-Point Accumulator Range
----------------------------------
fxd_accum_t = ap_fixed<56,38> is used only in QUANTIZE_LOOP (12× per MAC
call) and compute_output float conversion.  The main accumulation is in
dsp_acc_t = ap_int<48> (DSP48E2 P register), which has range ±140 trillion
— no overflow risk for real model data.

The narrow REDUCE multiply (ap_int<27> × ap_fixed<18,8> → 45-bit product
accumulated in 48-bit) has been validated through C-simulation.  T4 max_err
stays at ~0.63 (INT8 quantization bound), confirming no precision loss from
the 18-bit d representation vs the original 32-bit.


8. Resource Headroom — What Can't Be Used
-------------------------------------------
    DSP:    148 of 1,248 (12%)  — math only, can't replace routing LUTs
    URAM:     8 of    64 (12%)  — deep storage, can't replace pipeline FFs
    BRAM:  ~190 of   288 (66%)  — storage, used for nibble tiles and caches
    LUTRAM: ~4.5K of 57.6K (8%) — small distributed RAM, x_local only

The abundant DSPs and URAM are structurally mismatched to the design's
actual bottleneck: LUT-based routing (nibble muxes, AXI address computation,
DATAFLOW pipeline registers).  No clear path to trade DSPs/URAM for LUTs
in the critical paths.


9. Attempted Optimizations That Failed
----------------------------------------
    a. K_WV=16: MAC_ALL II=1 confirmed, 135K Vivado LUTs (115%).
       Reverted.  ZU5EV fabric handles 16-chain II=1 but LUT budget can't.

    b. K_DOWN=16: 130.8K Vivado LUTs (112%), REDUCE_MG routing inflation.
       Reverted.  16 parallel DSP routes in output path cause super-linear
       LUT growth in Vivado even with single-DSP multiplies.

    c. Output time-mux REDUCE: LUTs saved (8,418→3,187) but +295K cycles.
       Reverted.  REDUCE(64) > MAC_MG(35); overlap can't hide REDUCE time.

    d. Stream-based producer-consumer: FF bloat (250K, 106%) from hls::stream
       FIFO.  Replaced with direct ping-pong buffer DATAFLOW (221K FF, 94%).

    e. Output double-buffering: nested DATAFLOW duplicated load_mg hardware,
       163K Vivado LUTs.  Reverted to single-buffered output.

    2.    Overlap W_down DDR pre-load      ~200-400K      High — major

    approaching the Pareto frontier for ZU5EV Q4_0 SwiGLU acceleration.
